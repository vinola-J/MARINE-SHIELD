# Standard Operating Procedures for Coastal Cleanup Operations

**Document ID:** KB-DOC-005  
**Category:** Coastal Cleanup Guidance  
**Source:** International Shoreline Cleanup Protocol & Community Coastal Stewardship Standard  

## 1. Objectives of Coastal Cleanup Operations
Coastal cleanup operations aim to eliminate hazardous anthropogenic waste from marine intertidal habitats, prevent offshore drift of synthetic polymers, and catalog debris metrics to inform source-reduction policies.

## 2. Safety Protocols and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)
- **Mandatory PPE:** Heavy work gloves (nitrile or leather-palmed), closed-toe thick-soled footwear, sun protection (hat, UV-blocking sunglasses, SPF sunscreen), and adequate potable hydration fluids.
- **Handling Hazardous Materials:**
  - Never directly touch medical waste (syringes, IV tubing), unknown chemical drums, pyrotechnic marine flares, or pressurized gas canisters. Mark GPS location and alert municipal environmental hazmat responders immediately.
  - Sharp debris (broken glass, razor blades, rusted hooks) must be deposited in puncture-proof sharps containers or hard plastic buckets.
- **Tidal Awareness:** Always check local tide tables before cleanup operations. Work on an ebbing (falling) tide to maximize safe access to the wash zone and avoid being cut off by rising high tides.

## 3. Waste Categorization and Segregation at Source
Segregate collected debris into four distinct color-coded waste streams during field operations:
1. **Recyclable Rigid Plastics:** Beverage bottles, rigid jugs (HDPE/PET).
2. **Derelict Fishing Gear & Netting:** Ropes, lines, traps, monofilament nets.
3. **Metals & Glass:** Clean cans for scrap recycling; broken glass in rigid containers for safe disposal.
4. **General Non-Recyclable Trash:** Degraded plastic films, foam fragments, cigarette filters, contaminated mixed debris.

## 4. Post-Cleanup Data Reporting
- Record total volunteer person-hours, shoreline length covered in meters or kilometers, and total mass in kilograms.
- Utilize standardized marine debris tally sheets (e.g., Ocean Conservancy International Coastal Cleanup protocol) to submit field data for scientific tracking.
