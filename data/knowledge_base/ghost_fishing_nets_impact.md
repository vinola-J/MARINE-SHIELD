# Abandoned, Lost, and Discarded Fishing Gear (ALDFG) and Ghost Nets

**Document ID:** KB-DOC-002  
**Category:** Marine Litter Management  
**Source:** Global Ghost Gear Initiative (GGGI) Framework & Marine Fisheries Assessment Protocol  

## 1. Nature of Ghost Fishing Gear
Abandoned, Lost, or Discarded Fishing Gear (ALDFG), commonly termed "ghost gear" or "ghost nets", consists of nylon monofilament gillnets, trawls, longlines, and crab/lobster pots left adrift or snagged on benthic reefs. Because synthetic nylon and polyolefin fibers do not readily decompose, ghost nets continue to capture marine organisms indefinitely—a phenomenon called "ghost fishing."

## 2. Environmental and Ecological Significance
- **Lethal Entanglement:** Ghost nets represent one of the most acute mortal threats to marine wildlife. Entangled cetaceans (dolphins, whales), sea turtles, pinnipeds, and sea birds suffer prolonged constriction, drowning, severed flippers, and severe infection.
- **Benthic Habitat Smothering:** Derelict gear dragged across coral reefs, rocky outcrops, and seagrass meadows shears structural corals, dislodges sessile invertebrates, and smothers benthic filter feeders under sediment-trapping net bundles.
- **Continuous Mortality Cycle:** Captured fish decompose in the net, attracting scavengers (crabs, larger fish, sharks), which in turn become entangled, creating a self-sustaining cycle of wildlife loss.

## 3. Recommended Field Response and Safety
- **High Caution Alert:** Never pull heavily submerged or snagged nets alone. Submerged fishing nets can trap human hands, limbs, or diving gear under wave surge.
- **Assessing Entangled Fauna:** If live wildlife is entangled, immediately contact licensed marine animal rescue personnel or coastal wildlife authorities before cutting net strands. Inexperienced attempts to cut nets around panicked animals can result in severe injury to both rescuer and animal.
- **Extraction Tools:** Use specialized blunt-tip rescue line cutters rather than pointed knives. Secure tow lines to ensure the net does not drift away during manual cutting.
- **Disposal and Recycling:** Keep recovered nets separate from ordinary municipal waste; clean monofilament netting can often be routed to specialized nylon recycling partners or secure containment to prevent re-entry into waterways.

## 4. Monitoring Considerations
- Record GPS coordinates, mesh size (knot-to-knot distance), twine diameter, buoy markings, and estimated total net mass.
- Note whether the net was floating near the surface, intertidal, or anchored to benthic structures.
