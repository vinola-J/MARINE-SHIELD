# Coastal Marine Pollution Monitoring Protocols and Triage Assessment

**Document ID:** KB-DOC-006  
**Category:** Environmental Monitoring Guidance  
**Source:** Standardized Marine Litter Survey Methodology & Coastal Assessment Protocol  

## 1. Survey Methodologies: 100-Meter Transect Protocol
To assess coastal pollution rigorously, surveyors establish standard 100-meter transects running parallel to the water's edge, stretching from the current water line to the upper storm wrack line or foredune boundary.
- **Debris Density Metric:** Compute items per square meter ($items/m^2$) or items per 100 linear meters of coastline.
- **Size Classification:**
  - Micro-litter: $< 5\text{ mm}$
  - Meso-litter: $5\text{ mm} - 25\text{ mm}$
  - Macro-litter: $> 25\text{ mm}$ to $1\text{ meter}$
  - Mega-litter: $> 1\text{ meter}$ (e.g., shipping pallets, large abandoned nets)

## 2. Rapid Triage Severity Scoring
When full quantitative surveys are impossible, environmental responders apply a three-tiered rapid triage rating:
- **LOW Severity:** Isolated, single-item debris; no entangled or threatened wildlife; low risk to recreational users; minimal threat of immediate wave wash-away into pelagic currents.
- **MEDIUM Severity:** Clustered debris (5-20 items within a 10-meter radius) or presence of moderate hazard items (brittle plastic fragments, whole glass bottles, oxidized metal cans); moderate likelihood of wave-action dispersal.
- **HIGH Severity:** Dense debris accumulation ($>20$ items in close proximity), large derelict fishing gear (ghost nets), hazardous chemicals, sharp laceration risks over public walkways, or debris actively smothering sensitive habitats (coral reefs, sea turtle nesting grounds).

## 3. Incident Documentation and Evidence Preservation
- **Geotagged Photography:** Capture wide-angle contextual photographs showing the debris relative to the tide line and dunes, followed by close-up perpendicular images showing item composition and manufacturer markings.
- **Metadata Logging:** Record UTC timestamp, high/low tide state, ambient weather conditions (e.g., recent storm surge or king tide events), and substrate type (fine sand, cobble, mudflat, rocky reef).
- **Official Reporting:** Escalate high-severity alerts to local maritime environmental agencies, national marine sanctuary managers, or municipal coastal protection authorities.
