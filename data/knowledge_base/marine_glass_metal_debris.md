# Marine Glass and Metal Debris: Physical Hazards and Environmental Persistence

**Document ID:** KB-DOC-003  
**Category:** Coastal Litter Management  
**Source:** Coastal Debris Management Guide & Beach Safety Assessment Manual  

## 1. Overview of Glass and Metal Debris
Glass and metallic waste found on coastal shorelines primarily originate from beverage containers (aluminum cans, crown caps, glass beverage bottles), industrial packaging, food tins, boat fittings, and discarded construction materials. 

## 2. Environmental and Physical Hazards
- **Physical Injury Risk:** Broken glass shards and corroded tin cans with sharp serrated edges present an immediate laceration danger to beach visitors, barefoot recreational users, and coastal wildlife (such as nesting seabirds and seal colonies).
- **Entrapment of Small Organisms:** Narrow-necked bottles and open tins left intertidally frequently act as lethal pitfall traps for coastal hermit crabs, small lizards, and juvenile gastropods, which crawl inside and cannot escape.
- **Corrosion and Leachates:** Unlike glass (which is chemically inert, though physically hazardous), metallic debris undergoes galvanic and electrochemical corrosion in saline seawater. Rusty iron fragments release iron oxides, and degrading aluminum and galvanized steel alloys may release trace metallic ions into localized tidal sediment pore-water.

## 3. Recommended Response and Collection
- **Rigid Container Storage:** Never place broken glass fragments or jagged metal lids in thin plastic garbage bags, which tear easily and injure volunteers. Use heavy-duty puncture-proof plastic buckets or five-gallon pails for glass and metal collection.
- **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Handlers must wear heavy nitrile- or Kevlar-reinforced work gloves. Sturdy thick-soled boots are mandatory to prevent puncture wounds from buried glass shards.
- **Segregation:** Separate whole bottles and clean aluminum cans for community recycling programs. Encapsulate broken shards safely before final landfill disposal.

## 4. Monitoring and Documentation
- Document whether glass is whole or fragmented ("sea glass" with tumbled rounded edges poses lower acute injury risk than freshly broken sharp shards).
- Note brand and labeling to determine whether debris originates from recent shoreline visitor littering or maritime vessel overboard disposal.
