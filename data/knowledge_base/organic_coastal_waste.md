# Organic Coastal Waste, Macroalgal Inundation, and Biomass Dynamics

**Document ID:** KB-DOC-004  
**Category:** Environmental Monitoring Guidance  
**Source:** Coastal Estuarine and Wetland Ecology Monitoring Guidelines  

## 1. Description of Organic Marine Debris
Organic marine debris includes pelagic macroalgae (such as pelagic Sargassum fluitans and natans), wrack-line seagrass detritus, dead marine organisms, animal carcasses, and driftwood. While natural organic material plays a vital ecological role as nutrient sources and nesting habitat, abnormal excessive influxes disrupt coastal ecosystems.

## 2. Environmental and Ecological Significance
- **Anoxia and Eutrophication:** When massive volumes of seaweed or organic matter decompose along stagnant coastal lagoons or beaches, bacterial respiration consumes dissolved oxygen, creating localized anoxic (oxygen-depleted) dead zones that cause localized fish and invertebrate kills.
- **Toxic Gas Emissions:** Anaerobic decomposition of heavy organic mats generates hydrogen sulfide (H2S) gas and ammonia, causing respiratory irritation, nauseous odors, and potential health hazards for coastal residents and workers.
- **Smothering of Sea Turtle Nests:** Heavy carpets of washed-up sargassum block sea turtle hatchlings from crawling from dune nests into the open ocean, resulting in desiccation or predation.

## 3. Recommended Field Response and Management
- **Preserve Natural Wrack Line:** Do not mechanically strip normal, healthy wrack lines containing small amounts of dried kelp and driftwood, as these stabilize beach sand against erosion and nourish beach-dwelling amphipods.
- **Manage Excessive Accumulations:** In areas with heavy anoxic rotting biomass exceeding 30 cm depth, manual rake collection or low-impact agricultural raking may be deployed away from sensitive turtle nesting zones.
- **Protective Measures:** Cleanup workers near decomposing organic mats must wear masks or respirators if hydrogen sulfide odor is noticeable, along with eye protection and waterproof boots.

## 4. Monitoring Considerations
- Estimate thickness and coverage (percentage of intertidal zone covered).
- Verify whether plastic or synthetic debris is interwoven into the organic mass, requiring manual sorting prior to municipal composting or burial.
